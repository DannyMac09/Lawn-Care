## Chapter 1: What Bucket Golf Is (and What You Need)

Bucket golf is exactly what it sounds like: golf, but the hole is a bucket.

You stand at a tee marker, look downrange at a 5-gallon bucket with a flagstick poking out of it, and chip a golf ball at it. That's the whole game, and it's perfect — part golf, part cornhole, part tailgate. It works in a big backyard, a campground field, a tailgate lot, or any patch of open ground where a shanked wedge won't break a window. Kids can play it. Grandparents can play it. Your buddy who has never held a club can play it, and by hole three he'll be talking trash like he's on tour.

The game has been passed around campgrounds and cookouts for years, and every group plays it a little differently. This rulebook is the house standard: one clean, playable set of rules your whole crew can agree on before the first tee shot. Adopt it as-is, or tweak it — but agree on it first, because nothing ruins a cookout faster than a rules argument on hole seven.

### The Gear List

The beauty of bucket golf is that you probably own half of this already.

**Buckets.** One 5-gallon bucket per hole. The classic orange or white hardware-store bucket is the standard — it's the right size (about a foot across the mouth), it's nearly indestructible, and it makes a deeply satisfying *THONK* when a ball rattles in. For a 9-hole course, you need nine. If you're building a course on the cheap, ask around: somebody's garage has a stack of these.

**Flagsticks.** One per bucket. A flagstick is just a pole — 3 to 4 feet tall — stuck in the bucket so you can actually see your target from the tee. A wooden dowel, a length of PVC pipe, or a cheap driveway marker works fine. Tie a scrap of bright fabric or a bandana to the top as your flag. If you want the real look, grab a bucket golf flagstick — but a dowel and a red bandana play exactly the same.

**Balls.** Regular golf balls are the standard, and they fly true. But if your course is tight — neighbors close by, kids running around — use foam practice balls instead. They fly about half as far, they can't break anything, and a foam ball rattling into a bucket sounds just as good. A 2-pack per player is plenty; balls do get lost in bushes. Pick up some foam practice golf balls if your yard is on the cozy side.

**Clubs.** Here's the golden rule of bucket golf gear: **one club per player, for the whole round.** A pitching wedge or a high iron (a 9-iron or an 8-iron) is the perfect bucket golf club — enough loft to get the ball up and down quickly, enough control to aim at something a foot wide. Most players default to a wedge, and most house rules require it (see the one-club rule in Chapter 6). If your group has more players than clubs, thrift stores and garage sales are full of lone wedges for five bucks. That's not a compromise — that's the official equipment supplier of bucket golf.

**Tee markers.** Two per hole, to mark where you hit from. Golf tees stuck in the ground work, but they're hard to see. Better: a pair of small cones, two spray-painted rocks, or two short stakes. Anything visible from 30 yards that says "stand here."

**Scorecards and pencils.** You can absolutely keep score on a napkin, but a real scorecard makes it feel official. Chapter 5 has the layout; the league kit has a printable version.

**The cooler.** Not technically gear, but let's be honest — it's gear. Bucket golf is a social game. Drinks stay at the tee boxes (Chapter 7), and the cooler is the 19th hole between every hole.

Total startup cost if you own nothing: nine buckets, nine dowels, a handful of foam balls, and a thrift-store wedge — you can build a full course for less than a single round of real golf.

## Chapter 2: Building Your Course

A bucket golf course is a 9-hole layout you design around whatever ground you've got. There is no architect, no grounds crew, and no wrong answer — but a few measurements will take your course from "buckets scattered around the yard" to something that actually plays well.

### Tee Distances

Distance is the whole design. Too short and every hole is a tap-in; too long and nobody can reach and the game drags.

* **Standard holes: 15 to 30 yards.** This is the sweet spot. At 20 yards, a decent chip with a wedge lands soft near the bucket. At 30, you have to actually strike it. Mix your distances across the nine holes so the course has rhythm — short, medium, long, repeat.
* **Beginner or kid holes: 10 to 15 yards.** If little kids are playing, give them their own closer tee markers. They'll have more fun, and the game moves faster.
* **The signature long hole: 35 to 50 yards.** One per course, max. This is your par-4 showpiece — the one people talk about at the cooler. Anything past 50 yards stops being chipping and starts being a full swing, which is a safety problem in a backyard (Chapter 7).

Pace it off — one big adult stride is roughly a yard. Walk it heel-to-toe from the tee markers to the bucket, count your steps, and write the number on the scorecard. Nobody's bringing a laser rangefinder to a cookout.

### Hole Layout

Each hole has three parts: the tee, the fairway (whatever's in between — grass, dirt, the dog's favorite spot), and the bucket.

**Place the bucket first.** Set it upright on flat ground where a ball can actually stay near it — not on a slope where everything rolls 20 yards past. The flagstick goes in the bucket, flag up. Mark the bucket's spot with a small ring of spray paint or a flat stone, because buckets get knocked around and you'll want to put them back.

**Then walk back to the tee.** Set your two tee markers side by side, about a club-length apart. Players tee off from between or behind the markers — never in front of them.

**Aim your holes away from trouble.** This is the one real design rule: no hole should point at a road, a neighbor's windows, the grill, or the cooler. Angle holes so that the most likely miss (short and right, for most casual players) flies into open ground, not into the party.

**Space holes apart.** Buckets should sit at least 20 to 30 yards from the next hole's tee, and holes shouldn't cross each other. You don't want Group B's tee shots raining down on Group A's bucket. If your yard is tight, run the course as a loop around the perimeter — tee 1 near the house, buckets marching around the edges, hole 9 finishing back at the cooler.

### The 9-Hole Course Blueprint

Here's a ready-made layout for a typical big backyard or campground field. Distances are tee-to-bucket in yards:

| Hole | Yards | Par | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 18 | 3 | Gentle opener. Get loose. |
| 2 | 25 | 3 | First real test. |
| 3 | 14 | 3 | Shorty — birdie chance. |
| 4 | 30 | 3 | Demands a full, committed chip. |
| 5 | 22 | 3 | Dogleg around the oak tree (obstacle!). |
| 6 | 40 | 4 | The signature long hole. |
| 7 | 16 | 3 | Over (or around) the garden bed. |
| 8 | 28 | 3 | Slightly uphill if your yard allows. |
| 9 | 20 | 3 | Finisher, right back at the cooler. |

**Total: 213 yards, par 28.**

Use obstacles — trees, bushes, sheds, the swing set. A bucket tucked behind a tree that forces a high flop shot is infinitely more fun than nine buckets in a flat row. Just make sure the obstacle can't be damaged and can't damage a ball flying at it (no windows, no cars).

Walk the whole course once before anyone plays. Hit a ball on each hole yourself. If a hole feels unfair or unsafe, move the bucket. You're the course architect — act like it.

## Chapter 3: How to Play — Turn Order and Chipping

### The Objective

On every hole, chip your ball toward the bucket. Count every stroke. The hole ends the instant any ball strikes the bucket (or the flagstick) or drops in. Lowest total score after nine holes wins.

That's it. Everything else in this chapter is just the details that keep it fair.

### Turn Order

**Starting the round.** On hole 1, pick a teeing order however you like — draw straws, youngest first, or let the course architect (whoever built the course) take the honors. Write it down.

**After hole 1: honors.** The player with the lowest score on the previous hole tees off first on the next hole. If two players tied, the one who finished the previous hole first keeps the earlier spot — in other words, ties don't change the order. This is called "having the honors," and yes, you are absolutely allowed to announce it.

**Within a hole: farthest plays first.** Just like real golf — after everyone has teed off, the player whose ball is farthest from the bucket hits next. This keeps things safe (nobody's chipping while someone's standing near the bucket) and it's self-organizing: you can see who's away. When in doubt about who's farther, the players involved sort it out in two seconds. Don't overthink it.

### Chipping

Every shot in bucket golf is a chip — a short, controlled swing, not a full driver swing. Here's the 60-second technique that will have a total beginner hitting decent shots:

1. **Grip down.** Choke down on the club an inch or two from the top of the grip. Shorter club, more control.
2. **Narrow stance, ball back.** Feet close together, ball positioned slightly behind center — off your back foot. Weight leaning a little toward the target.
3. **Short swing, accelerate through.** Take the club back to about waist height and swing through. The biggest beginner mistake is decelerating — swinging hard at the ball and then quitting. Commit to the follow-through.
4. **Land it short.** Aim to land the ball a few yards short of the bucket and let it release toward it. Flying it all the way to the bucket looks heroic and works about one time in twenty.

**The golden rule of pace:** this is a social game. Take your shot within about 30 seconds of it being your turn. Line it up, hit it, laugh about it, move on. Nobody's reading a 20-yard chip like it's Augusta.

### When the Hole Ends

Play continues — farthest-first, stroke by stroke — until **a ball strikes the bucket or the flagstick, or drops into the bucket.** The instant that happens, the hole is over. Everyone stops. Scores are recorded as the number of strokes each player has taken so far.

This creates the game's central drama: **ending the hole can help your opponents.** If you rattle the bucket on your fourth shot while your buddy has only taken two, he just scored a 2 — and you're the one who gave it to him. Sometimes the smart play is to lag one close and let somebody else be the hero. Sometimes you can't help yourself and you fire at it anyway. That's bucket golf.

## Chapter 4: The Official-ish Rules

These are the house standard. Read them out loud before the first round — it takes two minutes and prevents 100% of cookout arguments.

**Rule 1 — One club.** Each player uses a single club for the entire round (a wedge or high iron). You pick it on hole 1 and you're married to it. (Your group can vote to allow club changes — see Variations — but one club is the default.)

**Rule 2 — The bucket ends the hole.** The hole is over the moment any ball strikes the bucket, strikes the flagstick, or comes to rest inside the bucket. There are no extra shots, no "one more for the road." Everyone's score is their stroke count at that moment.

**Rule 3 — Chip-in deduction.** If your ball goes INTO the bucket and stays in, subtract one stroke from your score for that hole. Rattling the bucket and having it bounce out still ends the hole — but only a ball that stays in earns the deduction.

**Rule 4 — Minimum score of 1.** The lowest possible score on a hole is 1. If you hole out from the tee in one shot, you score 1, not 0. The math doesn't go below 1, no matter how good the shot was.

**Rule 5 — Stroke cap.** Maximum score on any hole is double par (a par-3 caps at 6, a par-4 at 8). If you reach the cap, pick up your ball and take the cap. This keeps the game moving and protects everyone's dignity.

**Rule 6 — Out of bounds and lost balls.** If your ball goes somewhere unplayable — over the fence, into the pond, into the neighbor's yard you were told to stay out of — take a drop within two club-lengths of where it was last seen, add one penalty stroke, and play on. A ball that can't be found after a quick look is lost: same treatment.

**Rule 7 — Wrong bucket.** Only the bucket you're playing counts. If your ball hits a different hole's bucket, that's just an unlucky bounce — take free relief (drop within two club-lengths, no penalty) and keep playing. It does NOT end the hole.

**Rule 8 — The bucket stays put.** Buckets must be upright in their marked spots. If a bucket gets knocked over during a hole, stand it back up where it was before anyone hits. A ball that tips the bucket over counts as a bucket hit — the hole ends.

**Rule 9 — Flagstick stays in.** Play it as it lies: the flagstick stays in the bucket. A ball striking the flagstick counts exactly like striking the bucket — hole over.

**Rule 10 — No mulliganing the bucket.** (If your group plays mulligans — Chapter 6.) You cannot mulligan the shot that hit the bucket. The hole is over. Them's the breaks.

**Rule 11 — Play it as it lies.** No improving your lie, no kicking the ball closer when nobody's looking. We all see you, Dave.

**Rule 12 — The architect's word is law.** On any situation these rules don't cover, the person who built the course makes the call. Their decision is final, delivered with full mockery rights for everyone else.

## Chapter 5: Scoring and Scorecards

### Stroke Play

Bucket golf is scored like golf: count every stroke, add them up, lowest total wins. Your score on a hole is:

> **Strokes taken − 1 (if your ball stayed in the bucket) = hole score**
> 
> Minimum 1 per hole. Maximum double par per hole.

Example: you chip four times and your fourth shot drops in the bucket and stays. That's 4 − 1 = **3** on a par-3. A regulation par with a flourish.

Example: your buddy's second shot rattles the bucket but bounces out. Hole's over. He took 2 strokes, no deduction: **2**. You're furious because you'd only taken 1 stroke and just scored a 1 without doing anything. That's the game.

### Par

Par is the number of strokes a good player should need on a hole. Use the course blueprint from Chapter 2:

* **Par 3:** every hole from 10 to 35 yards.
* **Par 4:** holes from 36 to 50 yards.
* **Par 5:** anything over 50 yards (which you shouldn't build — see Chapter 2 — but if your field is enormous, go wild).

A standard 9-hole course of par-3s plays to **par 27**. With one long hole mixed in, par 28. Write the par for each hole on the scorecard before the round starts.

### Reading Your Score

Golfers talk in birdies and bogeys, and so should you:

* **1 under par:** birdie (or better — an ace from the tee is a hole-in-one, announce it loudly)
* **Even par:** par
* **1 over:** bogey
* **2 over:** double bogey
* **3 over:** triple bogey — at which point you hit the stroke cap anyway

### The Scorecard

Keep one card per group. Columns across the top: hole numbers 1–9, then OUT (front-nine total). Rows down the side: each player's name. Two extra rows at the top: **par** and **yards** for each hole, filled in before the round.

After each hole, one person calls out scores and writes them down while everyone watches. Verbal confirmation — "Mike 4, Sarah 3, Dave 6" — takes ten seconds and stops every dispute before it starts. At the end, total each column, lowest total wins, and the winner signs the card like it's the Masters.

Playing 18? Play the nine twice — second time through is the "back nine" (holes 10–18 on the card, same buckets). Total both nines for the full 18-hole score.

## Chapter 6: Popular Variations

The house rules in Chapters 4–5 are the standard. But bucket golf has always been a folk game, and half the fun is the house rules your crew invents. Here are the variations worth stealing — each one is playable, each one changes the game, and each one has been argued about at a cookout somewhere.

### The One-Club Rule (default: ON)

Everyone plays the entire round with a single club — almost always a wedge. This is the default in this rulebook because it levels the field: nobody gains an edge from a full bag, and it forces creativity. Your group can vote it off and allow club changes between holes, but try it the classic way first.

### Bucket-Ends-Hole (default: ON)

The standard: the hole ends the instant any ball strikes the bucket or flagstick. Some groups play a gentler version — **"everyone finishes"** — where after the bucket is hit, each other player gets one final shot. That version is friendlier but slower, and it kills the delicious cruelty of handing your buddy a 1. Pick one before the round.

### Chip-In Stroke Deduction (default: ON)

Ball stays in the bucket: minus one stroke. This is the rule that makes people attempt ridiculous shots, because a chip-in on a par-3 turns a 3 into a 2. Some hardcore groups play **double deduction** (minus two), which turns the game into a dunk contest. Fun for one round, chaos for a season.

### Mulligans (default: 2 per round)

Each player gets two mulligans per 9-hole round — a do-over on any stroke. Use it on a shank, a chunked chip, or the one that went backward. You cannot mulligan the shot that hit the bucket (Rule 10). Unused mulligans don't carry over, and no, you can't borrow Dave's. Announce your mulligan BEFORE you re-hit — retroactive mulligans are just cheating with extra steps.

### Team Scramble

The best format for big groups and beginners. Teams of 2 to 4 players. Everyone chips from the tee; the team picks the best shot; everyone plays their next shot from that spot. Repeat until somebody hits the bucket. The team's score is the number of team strokes. Scramble erases individual embarrassment — the worst player on the team still gets to be the hero once a round — and it's the fastest way to get a big group through nine holes.

### Skins

Each hole is worth one "skin." Lowest score on the hole wins the skin outright; if two or more tie for low, the skin carries over to the next hole. Most skins at the end wins. Skins can be worth bragging rights, a dollar each, or the loser buys the ice. Warning: carryovers create enormous pressure on hole 9. That's the point.

### Horse (the shootout)

No course needed — just one bucket. Players take turns chipping from an agreed spot; if you hit the bucket (or hole out, depending on the house call), everyone else has to match it or takes a letter. Spell H-O-R-S-E and you're out. Last player standing wins. Perfect for settling bets and killing time while the burgers cook.

### Night Golf

Same game, after dark. Drop a glow stick in each bucket, use glow-in-the-dark golf balls or wrap balls in reflective tape, and light the tee boxes with lanterns. Everything is harder and funnier at night. Mandatory: all the safety rules in Chapter 7, doubled.

## Chapter 7: Etiquette and Safety

Bucket golf involves swinging a metal stick at a ball near your friends. The vibes are immaculate and the injury risk is low — keep it that way.

### Spacing: Shanks Happen

* **Never stand in front of or beside the player hitting.** Stand behind them, off to the side, at least 5 yards back. Shanks go sideways. Chunks go anywhere.
* **Nobody stands near the bucket while someone is chipping at it.** Wait at the tee or off to the side until it's your turn. The one exception: one designated "bucket tender" may stand BEHIND the bucket (never in front of it) to confirm hits — and only if the whole group agrees.
* **Check your backswing.** Before you swing, glance behind you. Dogs, kids, and coolers all migrate toward golfers like iron filings to a magnet.
* **Yell "FORE!"** if a shot goes somewhere it shouldn't — toward another group, toward the party, toward the road. Loud and early. It's funny AND safe.

### Kids

Kids love bucket golf, and they should play — with two rules. **One:** they hit only from their closer tee markers (Chapter 2), which keeps them away from full-strength adult chips. **Two:** when it's not their turn, they wait at the tee with an adult. No wandering downrange to "check the bucket." Ever.

### Drinks

Drinks live at the tee boxes and the cooler — never near the buckets, never in the landing zone. A spilled drink is a tragedy; a shattered bottle near bare feet is a hospital visit. Cans and plastic only on the course. Glass stays at the patio.

### The Course

* **Get permission.** If the course isn't on your property — a campground field, a park, a buddy's yard — ask first. Fill your divots (press the turf back down), pick up every ball, and leave the ground better than you found it.
* **Respect the buckets.** They're the course. Don't sit on them, don't use them as trash cans mid-round, and put them back in their marked spots if they move.
* **Pace of play.** Thirty seconds per shot, stroke cap enforced, no practice swings beyond one. A foursome should finish nine holes in about an hour. If your group is slower than that, you're not playing bucket golf — you're holding a meeting.

### The Spirit of the Game

Call your own penalties. Congratulate the good shots, especially your opponents'. The trash talk should be about the swing, not the person (the league kit has a whole code of conduct on this). And when somebody dunks one from 25 yards out — stop, applaud, and make them tell the story twice. That's what the game's for.

## Quick-Start Cheat Sheet

*New players: read this page and you're ready. Everything else is details.*

**GEAR:** One 5-gallon bucket + flagstick per hole. One wedge per player. Golf balls (or foam if the yard is tight). Tee markers. Scorecard.

**SETUP:** 9 holes, 15–30 yards each. Buckets upright on flat ground, flagsticks in. Tee markers set back from each bucket. Holes aimed away from roads, windows, and the party.

**TURN ORDER:** Lowest score on the last hole tees first. Within a hole, farthest from the bucket hits first.

**PLAY:** Chip at the bucket. Count every stroke. Farthest-first until someone hits it.

**THE BUCKET ENDS THE HOLE.** Ball hits the bucket or flagstick → hole over, everyone takes their stroke count. Ball stays IN the bucket → minus 1 stroke. Lowest possible hole score: 1.

**CAP:** Max score per hole is double par (6 on a par-3). Reach it, pick up.

**PENALTIES:** Lost ball or unplayable: drop within 2 club-lengths, +1 stroke. Wrong bucket: free drop, no penalty, doesn't end the hole.

**MULLIGANS:** 2 per round. Not on the bucket shot.

**WINNING:** Lowest total after 9 holes. Ties → sudden-death chip-off (Chapter 6 in the League Kit... or just play another hole).

**SAFETY:** Stand behind the hitter. Nobody near the bucket during shots. Kids at the tee. Drinks at the tee. Yell FORE.

*Now go build the course. First tee is in 20 minutes.*
