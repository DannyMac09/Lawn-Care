## Chapter 1: Why Run a League

Casual bucket golf is great. League bucket golf is better, for exactly one reason: **stakes change everything.**

The moment there's a standings board on somebody's fridge, a 20-yard chip stops being a casual knock-around and starts being a moment. People practice. People show up on time. People develop rivalries, superstitions, and strong opinions about the pin placement on hole 6. A league turns "we should play sometime" into "see you Thursday" — and Thursday becomes the best night of the week.

Here's what a league gives you that pickup games don't:

**A reason to get better.** Handicaps (Chapter 3) mean the beginner and the ringer compete on equal terms, so everyone improves and nobody gets embarrassed off the course. Players who start the season chunking every third chip end it lagging putts — sorry, chips — like they've been doing it for years.

**Built-in social life.** A weekly league is a standing appointment with your favorite people. No group-chat herding, no "who's free Saturday?" — the schedule says Thursday at 6:30, and everybody knows it.

**Stories.** Every league produces them: the 40-yard walk-off bucket hit in the semifinal, the commissioner who lost the trophy, the rookie who won the whole thing on a chip-in. Pickup games are forgotten by Monday. League moments get retold for years.

**An ending.** Seasons end with playoffs and a champion, which means every season gets a climax — and every climax sets up next season's revenge tour.

You don't need a big group. Four players is a league. Eight is the sweet spot. Sixteen and you're running a small empire. Whatever you've got, this kit scales to fit — and by Chapter 6, you'll have a tournament day that feels like an event.

## Chapter 2: Season Format — Weeks, Matchups, and Points

### The Standard Season: 6 Weeks + Playoffs

The format that works for nearly every backyard league:

* **Weeks 1–6:** regular season. Everybody plays 9 holes each week, same course (or rotating host courses — see below).
* **Week 7:** playoffs. Top 4 or top 8 make the bracket (Chapter 4).
* **Week 8 (optional):** championship + awards cookout.

Six weeks is long enough to matter and short enough that nobody's schedule falls apart. If your crew wants more, run 8 or 10 weeks — the structure doesn't change.

### Two Ways to Score the Season

Pick ONE before week 1. Don't mix them mid-season unless you enjoy arguments.

**Option A: Points race (stroke play).** Every week, players are ranked by their 9-hole score (net score if you're using handicaps — Chapter 3). Points are awarded by finish:

| Finish | Points (8-player league) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1st | 10 |
| 2nd | 8 |
| 3rd | 6 |
| 4th | 5 |
| 5th | 4 |
| 6th | 3 |
| 7th | 2 |
| 8th | 1 |

For a different field size, scale it: 1st place gets (players + 2) points, 2nd gets (players), 3rd gets (players − 2), then count down by 1 for each lower finish. Ties split the points for the tied positions — two players tied for 2nd in an 8-player league each get 7 (the average of 8 and 6).

**Option B: Head-to-head matchups.** Each week, players are paired off (random draw, or seeded by standings). Each matchup is a 9-hole stroke-play match: win = 2 points, tie = 1 point each, loss = 0. With an odd number of players, one player gets a bye each week (1 point, and they still play their round for handicap purposes).

The points race is simpler and better for casual groups. Head-to-head creates rivalries and weekly drama — better for competitive crews. Both work with handicaps.

### The Drop-Week Rule

Life happens. Count each player's **best 5 of 6** weeks toward the final standings. Miss a week? That's your drop. Have one catastrophic round? Dropped. This single rule does more for league harmony than anything else in this book — nobody quits in week 4 because they had one bad night.

### Rotating Courses

If multiple players have yards big enough for a course, rotate the host each week. It keeps things fresh, spreads the setup work, and stops one player from having an unbeatable home-course advantage. Each host sets their own 9-hole layout using the course-building rules in the Rulebook — but every course must publish its par and yardages before week 1 so handicaps stay fair.

### Season Standings Tiebreakers

If two players finish the regular season tied on points:

1. Head-to-head record (if you used matchups).
2. Lowest total net strokes across counting weeks.
3. Most weekly wins.
4. A 3-ball chip-off: one chip each from 20 yards, closest to the bucket takes it (Chapter 6 has the full chip-off procedure).

Post the standings every week — group chat, fridge, wherever the crew looks. A standings board that doesn't move is a league that dies.

## Chapter 3: Setting Handicaps (The Simple Way)

A handicap lets the beginner and the ringer compete in the same league without anyone getting embarrassed. The concept is simple: **your handicap is the number of strokes you're expected to need above par, and it's subtracted from your gross score to get your net score.** Lower net wins.

This system is designed so anyone can run it with a pencil. No apps, no spreadsheets required (though a spreadsheet doesn't hurt).

### Step 1: Establish the Handicap (3 Rounds)

Every player needs **three scored 9-hole rounds** to earn a handicap. These can be preseason practice rounds or the first three weeks of the season played at scratch (no handicaps yet).

The formula:

> **Handicap = 0.8 × (average gross score − course par), rounded to the nearest whole number**

Example: Sarah's three rounds are 35, 38, and 35 on a par-27 course. Her average is 36. That's 9 over par. 0.8 × 9 = 7.2, which rounds to **7**. Sarah's handicap is 7.

Example: Mike shoots 29, 30, 31 — average 30, just 3 over par. 0.8 × 3 = 2.4 → **2**. Mike's handicap is 2.

The 0.8 multiplier (instead of the full difference) keeps sandbaggers honest and rewards actual improvement — you have to beat your average to win, not just match it.

### Step 2: Apply It

**Net score = gross score − handicap.** If Sarah shoots 36 with a 7 handicap, her net is 29. If Mike shoots 31 with a 2, his net is 29. Tie — and that's the system working exactly as designed.

Use net scores for weekly points, head-to-head matchups, and playoff matches. Gross scores still get recorded (that's how handicaps update).

### The Rules Around It

* **Cap it at 12.** Nobody gets more than 12 strokes on a 9-hole course. Past that, the math starts handing out wins instead of leveling the field.
* **Minimum 0.** If you average under par, congratulations — your handicap is 0. Scratch golfer. Everyone's gunning for you now.
* **Recompute every 3 weeks.** After the initial handicap, recalculate using the player's most recent 6 rounds (or all rounds if fewer). Post the updated list before the next week. Players trending down get their handicap cut — that's the price of getting good.
* **New players mid-season:** they play scratch (handicap 0) until they log 3 rounds. No exceptions, no estimates. Three rounds goes fast.
* **Different courses:** if the league rotates courses (Chapter 2), use each course's own par in the formula. A 36 on a par-27 and a 38 on a par-30 are both 9 over — same handicap math.

### Worked Example: The Whole League

Par-27 course. After three weeks:

| Player | R1 | R2 | R3 | Avg | Over Par | × 0.8 | Hdcp |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mike | 29 | 30 | 31 | 30 | 3 | 2.4 | 2 |
| Sarah | 35 | 38 | 35 | 36 | 9 | 7.2 | 7 |
| Dave | 41 | 44 | 41 | 42 | 15 | 12.0 | 12 |
| Priya | 33 | 34 | 35 | 34 | 7 | 5.6 | 6 |

Week 4, they shoot: Mike 30, Sarah 35, Dave 40, Priya 34.
Net: Mike 28, Sarah 28, Dave 28, Priya 28. A four-way tie — and every single one of them earned it. That's a handicap system doing its job.

## Chapter 4: Single and Double Elimination Brackets

Playoffs are where legends are made. This chapter describes both bracket formats so clearly you can draw them on poster board with a marker — which is exactly what you should do, because a big visible bracket on tournament day is half the atmosphere.

### Seeding

Seed players by final regular-season standings: 1st place is the #1 seed, 2nd is #2, and so on. Standard tournament seeding pairs the best against the worst: in an 8-player bracket, the quarterfinals are **1 vs 8, 2 vs 7, 3 vs 6, 4 vs 5.** This rewards the regular season — the #1 seed earned the easiest first match.

For 4 players: semifinals are **1 vs 4, 2 vs 3**, winners meet in the final.

### Every Playoff Match Works the Same

Each match is **9 holes of stroke play, net scores** (gross minus handicap). Low net wins. Tie after 9? **Chip-off** (full procedure in Chapter 6): each player chips one ball from 20 yards at a designated bucket; closest to the bucket wins the match. Still tied? Keep chipping — sudden death — until somebody's closer.

### Single Elimination: The Classic

Lose once, go home. Brutal, simple, perfect.

**How to draw it (8 players):** On your poster board, draw two columns of four match boxes on the left — these are the quarterfinals. Label them QF1 through QF4 and write in the seeds: QF1 = 1v8, QF2 = 4v5, QF3 = 2v7, QF4 = 3v6. To the right, draw two semifinal boxes: the winner of QF1 plays the winner of QF2; the winner of QF3 plays the winner of QF4. To the right of those, one big box: THE FINAL. Draw arrows from each box to the next. Write winners' names in as matches finish.

**Schedule:** Quarterfinals in the morning (two matches can share a course — stagger tee times 30 minutes apart), semifinals after lunch, final as the showcase event with everyone watching. A full 8-player single-elim bracket takes one long day or two relaxed sessions.

**Third-place match (optional but recommended):** the two semifinal losers play 9 holes for 3rd. It gives everyone one more match and settles the "who's actually third" argument forever.

### Double Elimination: The Gauntlet

Lose once, drop to the losers' bracket. Lose twice, you're out. Nobody's season ends on one bad bounce — but the road back is long.

**How to draw it (8 players):** You need two bracket columns side by side.

* **Winners' bracket (left):** identical to single elimination — QF, SF, and a winners' bracket final. But losers don't go home; they drop down.
* **Losers' bracket (right):** draw it as a second column. Round 1 of the losers' bracket: the four quarterfinal losers pair off into two matches (top-half QF losers play each other, bottom-half QF losers play each other). The losers of these matches are eliminated — that's their second loss. The winners survive with one loss each and go on to face the two losers of the winners'-bracket semifinals (two matches). Those two winners play each other, and the survivor is the losers'-bracket champion.
* **Grand final:** winners'-bracket champion vs losers'-bracket champion. Here's the twist — **the bracket reset:** the winners'-bracket champ has never lost, so the challenger has to beat them TWICE (two 9-hole matches) to take the title. If the winners'-bracket champ wins the first match, it's over. If the challenger wins, play one more winner-take-all match. Announce this rule before the tournament. It is the most dramatic rule in amateur sports.

**Which to choose?** Single elimination for a one-day tournament — it's clean and finishes on time. Double elimination when you've got a full weekend and a crew that wants maximum golf. Double elim with 8 players needs roughly twice the matches, so plan accordingly.

### Bracket Tips

* **Byes:** with 5–7 players in single elim, give the top seeds byes into the semifinals rather than playing awkward play-in games. With 6 players: seeds 1 and 2 get byes; 3v6 and 4v5 play quarterfinals.
* **Post it big.** A [AFFILIATE: dry-erase tournament bracket board] on an easel at the course beats a phone screen. Update it in thick marker as matches finish. People will photograph it.
* **The commissioner's call is final** on all bracket disputes — seeding errors, no-shows (a no-show forfeits; their opponent advances), and weather delays.

## Chapter 5: The Scorecard — Printable Layout

A league scorecard does more than a casual one: it tracks gross score, handicap, and net score for every player, plus course info at the top. Here's the exact layout to draw on paper or build in a document — one card per group, per round.

### The Grid

Draw a table. Columns across the top, in this order:

**HOLE | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | OUT | HDCP | NET**

Rows down the left side, in this order:

1. **YDS** — yardage of each hole, filled in before the round.
2. **PAR** — par of each hole, filled in before the round.
3. One row per player: **player name**, then their gross score per hole.
4. (Optional footer row: **group best** — the low score per hole, for Skins games.)

The OUT column is the 9-hole gross total. HDCP is the player's handicap. NET = OUT − HDCP.

Here's what a filled-in card looks like:

```
BACKYARD BUCKET GOLF LEAGUE — Week 4 — Pine Street Course — 6/12

HOLE | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | OUT | HDCP | NET
YDS  |18 |25 |14 |30 |22 |40 |16 |28 |20 | 213|     |
PAR  | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 |  28|     |
Mike | 3 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 3 |  30|   2 | 28
Sarah| 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 3 |  33|   7 | 26
Dave | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 6 | 4 | 4 | 4 |  39|  12 | 27
Priya| 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 |  33|   6 | 27
```

Sarah wins the week on net 26. Dave's gross 39 looks rough until the handicap does its job — net 27, one stroke back. That's the whole league in one card.

### Drawing It on Paper

On a standard sheet, turn it landscape. You'll fit 8–10 player rows comfortably. Use a ruler — a crooked card is a disputed card. One player per group is the designated scorer; scores are called out loud after each hole ("Mike 3, Sarah 4...") and confirmed before anyone tees off on the next hole. At the end of the round, every player initials their own row. No initials, no disputes later.

### 18 Holes and Multi-Course Notes

* **18 holes:** play the 9 twice. Use two cards (or front and back of one sheet): OUT for the first nine, IN for the second nine, TOTAL = OUT + IN. Handicap applies once to the 18-hole total — double the 9-hole handicap.
* **Rotating courses:** write the course name and date at the top of every card, always. When handicaps get recomputed, the commissioner needs to know which par each round was played against.
* **Keep every card.** The commissioner files them — a shoebox works. They're the official record for handicaps, disputes, and end-of-season awards (most birdies, lowest round, biggest comeback).

Want to skip the ruler? Grab a pad of [AFFILIATE: golf scorecard pads] and write HDCP and NET columns in the margins — close enough for league play.

## Chapter 6: Tournament Day Runbook

This is the big one — playoffs, championship, the day the season builds toward. Run it like an event and it'll feel like one.

### Setup Checklist (Commissioner)

**Course gear:**
- [ ] 9 buckets, upright, in marked spots
- [ ] 9 flagsticks with flags
- [ ] Tee markers for every hole (plus kid tees if needed)
- [ ] Yardages and pars posted (whiteboard or printed sheet at hole 1)

**Play gear:**
- [ ] Balls — at least 2 per player, plus spares
- [ ] Clubs — confirm everyone has their one club
- [ ] Scorecards + pencils (Chapter 5 layout, one per group)
- [ ] Bracket poster + thick markers (Chapter 4)
- [ ] Printed season standings + current handicaps

**Comfort and safety:**
- [ ] Cooler stocked (drinks at tee boxes — Rulebook Chapter 7)
- [ ] Trash bags (leave the ground better than you found it)
- [ ] First-aid kit (blisters and stubbed toes are the main hazards)
- [ ] Sunscreen and bug spray to share
- [ ] Prizes (see below)

**Nice touches:**
- [ ] Bluetooth speaker for the awards area
- [ ] Phone/camera charged — somebody films the final
- [ ] Grill going for the cookout after

### The Schedule (8-Player Single-Elim Tournament Day)

| Time | What |
| :--- | :--- |
| 9:00 AM | Setup. Commissioner + early volunteers place buckets, post pars, hang bracket. |
| 10:00 AM | Check-in. Confirm handicaps, hand out scorecards, announce any local rules. |
| 10:15 AM | Warm-up chips. 15 minutes, one bucket, Horse optional. |
| 10:30 AM | Quarterfinals tee off. Two matches per wave, staggered 30 min. (1v8 and 4v5 first.) |
| ~12:00 PM | Lunch. Grill, bracket update, trash talk. |
| 12:45 PM | Semifinals tee off. |
| ~2:00 PM | Third-place match tees off (optional). |
| 2:15 PM | **THE FINAL.** Everyone follows the final group. Gallery rules: silence during chips, eruption after bucket hits. |
| ~3:15 PM | Awards cookout. Trophies, stories, season recap. |

A foursome plays 9 holes in about an hour; head-to-head matches with two players go faster. Build in buffer — the final always runs long because nobody wants it to end.

### Tiebreakers: The Chip-Off Procedure

Any tied match or tied standings position gets settled by chip-off:

1. Pick a neutral hole — one neither player just played, or a dedicated chip-off bucket at 20 yards.
2. Each player gets **one ball, one chip**, alternating shots. Farthest from the bucket in the standings chips first.
3. **Closest to the bucket wins.** Measure from the ball's resting spot to the base of the bucket. A ball IN the bucket beats everything.
4. Still tied? Sudden death — keep chipping, one ball each, until somebody's closer.
5. The gallery may heckle. That's not a rule, it's just what happens.

### Prizes on a Budget (Under $50 Total)

Nobody needs expensive prizes. They need prizes with *meaning.*

* **The Golden Bucket (champion):** a thrift-store trophy or an actual small bucket, spray-painted gold. The champion keeps it until next season — then they have to defend it or hand it over. A [AFFILIATE: metallic gold spray paint] and a $3 trophy become the most contested object in your friend group.
* **The Shank Award (last place):** a plastic toy golf club, a whoopee cushion, whatever's funny. Awarded with ceremony. The winner of the Shank Award is contractually obligated to display it prominently until next season.
* **Closest-to-the-bucket (season):** for the best single chip-off or the lowest single round. A sleeve of real golf balls works.
* **Most improved:** biggest handicap drop from week 1 to playoffs. This is the award that keeps beginners coming back.
* **The commissioner's award:** for the best trash talk, the worst shank, the funniest moment — commissioner's choice, announced last, gets the biggest laugh.

Wrap it all in the awards cookout. Hand out prizes in reverse order — Shank Award first, Golden Bucket last, with a speech. Film it. This is the content the group chat runs on all winter.

## Chapter 7: The Trash-Talk Code of Conduct

Trash talk is the lifeblood of a bucket golf league. It's also the thing most likely to ruin one. This code keeps it fun — post it where everyone can see it, read it on opening day, and enforce it exactly once. After that, the culture enforces itself.

### The Code

**1. Roast the swing, not the person.** "That chip had the grace of a falling shed" — perfect. Anything about somebody's appearance, family, job, or life — out of bounds. The line is bright and everybody knows where it is.

**2. Silence during the swing.** You can heckle the *result* all you want. But while somebody is standing over the ball, the gallery goes quiet. Talking during a backswing isn't trash talk — it's just rude, and it's a one-stroke penalty if the group votes it so.

**3. The commissioner has the mute button.** If somebody's trash talk crosses the line or just won't stop, the commissioner can bench them for a hole — they sit out, score the cap for the hole, and think about what they did. Used once a season, it never needs using again.

**4. Losers talk loudest — after the round.** Mid-round chirping at somebody who's struggling is punching down. Save it for the cooler, where everyone's equal and the stories are better anyway.

**5. No excuses, no scoreboard amnesia.** "The sun was in my eyes" is fine once. A season of excuses is worse than losing. And when you lose, you say "good round" like you mean it — because next week you're coming for revenge, and revenge needs a willing opponent.

**6. The group chat is sacred.** What's said at the course stays in the crew. No posting somebody's shank video publicly without their okay. Inside the group chat? All bets are off — that's what it's for.

**7. Handshake at the end.** Win or lose, every match ends with a handshake (or a hug, or a club-tap — your crew's call). The rivalry resumes next week. The friendship doesn't pause.

### Why This Matters

A league without trash talk is just exercise. A league with *bad* trash talk is a group chat that slowly goes quiet. The code isn't about being soft — the best leagues talk the most trash. It's about making sure the rookie who joined in week 3 is still there in week 6, laughing, giving as good as they get. Protect the fun, and the fun compounds.
