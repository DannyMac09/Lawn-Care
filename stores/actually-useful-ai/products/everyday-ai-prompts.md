## How to use this book

Pick a prompt, copy it into ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or any AI assistant, and change the details in brackets to match your life. Each one includes a "When to use this" line so you can find the right prompt fast. You don't need to use them word for word — tweak them, combine them, and always follow up ("make it shorter," "explain that again") until the answer fits.

## Home (Prompts 1–20)

### 1
```
Write a polite but firm email to my landlord asking them to fix a dripping kitchen faucet. Mention it's been leaking for a week and my water bill is going up. Keep it short.
```
When to use this: Something in your rental needs fixing and you want it in writing without sounding rude.

### 2
```
Plan 5 weeknight dinners for 2 adults using what I already have: chicken breasts, rice, canned tomatoes, frozen broccoli, pasta, and eggs. Each meal must take under 30 minutes. Then list only the extra groceries I need to buy.
```
When to use this: You're staring into the fridge with no plan and don't want another takeout night.

### 3
```
Make me a realistic weekly cleaning schedule for a working parent with 2 kids. Split tasks across the week so nothing takes more than 20 minutes a day, and give the kids 2 age-appropriate jobs each (ages 7 and 10).
```
When to use this: The house is always messy and "clean the house" feels too big to start.

### 4
```
Walk me through deep-cleaning a bathroom step by step, in order. Assume I have basic supplies: all-purpose cleaner, bleach, a sponge, an old toothbrush, and rubber gloves. Tell me what to do first, second, third, so I don't waste time.
```
When to use this: The bathroom needs a real scrub and you want a clear order to follow.

### 5
```
Help me declutter my garage. Break it into 4 weekend sessions, tell me what to tackle in each session, and give me a simple keep / donate / toss rule to decide on each item fast.
```
When to use this: The garage is full of stuff and you keep putting off sorting it.

### 6
```
Make a packing list for a 7-day beach vacation for 2 adults and 1 toddler. Group it by category (clothes, toiletries, baby stuff, beach gear, documents) so I can check things off as I pack.
```
When to use this: You're packing for a trip and terrified of forgetting something important.

### 7
```
I'm moving in 8 weeks. Make me a week-by-week moving checklist starting now: what to do, who to notify, and when to start packing each room. Include the easy-to-forget stuff like the post office and the DMV.
```
When to use this: You're moving and want a countdown plan so nothing sneaks up on you.

### 8
```
Give me a simple home maintenance calendar: one short list of things to check or do each season (spring, summer, fall, winter) for a typical single-family house. Nothing technical — just what to look at and why.
```
When to use this: You want to take care of your house but never remember what needs doing when.

### 9
```
My bathroom sink drains very slowly. Explain the simplest safe fixes I can try myself before calling a plumber, in order from easiest to hardest. Tell me clearly what NOT to do so I don't damage the pipes.
```
When to use this: Something minor breaks and you want to try the easy fix before paying for a plumber.

### 10
```
Write clear pet-care instructions I can leave for a friend watching my dog for a weekend. Include feeding times and amounts, walk schedule, where the leash and food are, the vet's number as [VET NUMBER], and 3 house rules.
```
When to use this: Someone's watching your pet and you want everything written down in one place.

### 11
```
I'm buying a new refrigerator and I'm confused by the options. Explain in plain English what the main types are (top freezer, bottom freezer, side-by-side, French door), the pros and cons of each, and which suits a family of 4 that cooks most nights.
```
When to use this: You're shopping for a big appliance and the choices all blur together.

### 12
```
Plan the food for a backyard cookout for 12 adults and 6 kids. Tell me how much of each thing to buy (burgers, hot dogs, buns, sides, drinks, ice) so I don't run out or overbuy, and suggest 2 easy desserts I can make ahead.
```
When to use this: You're hosting a crowd and have no idea how much food "enough" is.

### 13
```
Make me a simple laundry routine: how often to do it for a family of 4, how to sort loads so colors don't bleed, what water temperature to use for what, and what absolutely should not go in the dryer.
```
When to use this: Laundry piles up constantly or someone keeps shrinking/ruining clothes.

### 14
```
My grandkids (ages 2 and 4) are visiting for a weekend. Give me a room-by-room childproofing checklist for a house that isn't set up for toddlers, focusing on the quick fixes I can do in one afternoon.
```
When to use this: Little kids are coming over and your house isn't kid-proof.

### 15
```
List exactly what should go in a basic home emergency kit for a family of 4: enough for 3 days without power. Be specific about quantities (how many gallons of water, what kind of food) and keep the total cost reasonable.
```
When to use this: Storm season is coming (or you just want to be prepared) and you need a concrete shopping list.

### 16
```
I want to repaint my living room. Tell me how to figure out how much paint to buy for a 12x15 foot room, what supplies I need beyond the paint, and the steps in order so the job looks professional, not patchy.
```
When to use this: You're painting a room for the first time and don't want to buy too much, too little, or mess it up.

### 17
```
Write a warm thank-you note for a housewarming gift (a set of kitchen towels) from my aunt. Make it sound personal, mention I'll think of her every time I use them, and keep it to about 4 sentences.
```
When to use this: You received a gift and want to send a thank-you that sounds like you, not a card company.

### 18
```
Write a polite note I can leave for my neighbor about their dog barking late at night. Keep it friendly — I like them and want to stay on good terms — but be clear that it's waking us up and ask if they can bring the dog in after 10pm.
```
When to use this: A neighbor issue is bothering you and you want to handle it kindly before it becomes a feud.

### 19
```
Plan a Saturday backyard barbecue for 15 people from 2pm to 7pm. Give me a timeline: when to start the grill, when to put food out, 3 easy lawn games to set up, and a rain backup plan.
```
When to use this: You're hosting outdoors and want a simple timeline so the day runs itself.

### 20
```
Give me a fall home winterizing checklist: the 10 most important things to do before cold weather hits (pipes, furnace, gutters, drafts), explained simply, with which ones I can do myself and which need a pro.
```
When to use this: Winter is coming and you want the house ready before the first freeze.

## Money (Prompts 21–40)

### 21
```
Help me build a monthly budget. I take home $3,400 a month, and my bills are: rent $1,150, car $310, insurance $165, phone $75, groceries $550, gas $200. I want to save $300 a month — show me where the money goes and what's left.
```
When to use this: You want a clear picture of your money instead of guessing where it goes.

### 22
```
I have three debts: a $2,400 credit card at 24% interest, a $5,800 credit card at 19%, and a $9,000 car loan at 7%. I can put an extra $200 a month toward debt. Explain in plain English which order to pay them off and why, and roughly how long it will take.
```
When to use this: You're carrying multiple debts and want a payoff order that actually makes sense.

### 23
```
Write me a word-for-word script for calling my internet provider to ask for a lower bill. My bill just went from $70 to $95. Include what to say if they say no the first time, and when to politely ask for a supervisor.
```
When to use this: A bill jumped up and you want to negotiate it down without freezing up on the call.

### 24
```
Help me compare two job offers: Offer A is $58,000 salary, 2 weeks vacation, $200/month health insurance, 30-minute commute; Offer B is $52,000 salary, 3 weeks vacation, free health insurance, work from home 3 days a week. Which is actually better once you count everything, not just salary?
```
When to use this: You're choosing between job offers and salary alone doesn't tell the whole story.

### 25
```
Explain emergency funds in plain English: how much a family of 4 with a mortgage should aim to save, where to keep it (regular savings vs. other options), and how to build it up $100 at a time without feeling deprived.
```
When to use this: You know you "should" have savings for emergencies but don't know where to start.

### 26
```
Here are my monthly expenses: [paste your list]. Find 5 realistic ways I could cut at least $150/month total without giving up everything fun. Be specific — no generic advice like "stop buying coffee."
```
When to use this: Money feels tight and you want concrete cuts, not lectures.

### 27
```
Explain credit scores like I'm hearing about them for the first time: what the number means, what makes it go up, what makes it go down, and the 3 most effective things I can do in the next 6 months to raise mine.
```
When to use this: Your credit score confuses you and you want to improve it with a real plan.

### 28
```
I want to buy a used car for under $12,000 cash within 6 months. Make me a savings plan: how much to save each month, plus a checklist of what to check before buying a used car so I don't get ripped off.
```
When to use this: You're saving for a big purchase and want the money plan plus the street smarts.

### 29
```
My roommate and I split rent and utilities, but it never feels fair. Our rent is $1,400, electric averages $130, internet is $60, and I have the bigger bedroom. Suggest a fair split and a simple system for tracking shared costs each month.
```
When to use this: Shared bills are causing friction and you want a fair system in writing.

### 30
```
I need to buy Christmas gifts for 9 people with a total budget of $400. Help me divide the budget fairly (closer family gets more), and suggest 2 specific gift ideas per person under their amount based on: [describe each person in one line].
```
When to use this: The holidays are coming and you want thoughtful gifts without the January credit card shock.

### 31
```
Explain a pay stub in plain English. My gross pay is $1,850 every two weeks but I only get $1,420. Tell me what each common deduction is (federal tax, Social Security, Medicare, state tax) and why the numbers are what they are.
```
When to use this: Your paycheck is smaller than expected and you want to know where the money went.

### 32
```
I want to take my family of 4 to the beach next summer and I think it'll cost about $2,500. I have 10 months. Make me a monthly savings plan, and suggest 3 painless ways to find the extra money each month.
```
When to use this: You're dreaming about a vacation and want a real plan to pay for it.

### 33
```
Help me audit my subscriptions. Here's what I pay for monthly: [list them]. Tell me which ones overlap, which ones I might be able to get cheaper (annual plans, family plans, bundles), and how much I'd save per year by cutting or downgrading.
```
When to use this: Subscriptions are quietly draining your account and you want to see the real total.

### 34
```
My job offers a 401(k) with a 50% company match up to 6% of my salary. Explain in plain English what that actually means, how much free money I'd leave on the table if I only contribute 3%, and what to do first if I'm confused.
```
When to use this: Retirement plan paperwork confuses you and you suspect you're missing out on free money.

### 35
```
I'm shopping for car insurance and got 3 quotes. Give me a checklist of what to compare beyond the price: coverage types explained simply, what the numbers like 100/300/100 mean, and red flags that a cheap policy is too cheap.
```
When to use this: Insurance quotes all look the same and you don't want to pick wrong just to save $10.

### 36
```
Write a firm but polite letter disputing a $180 charge on my medical bill for a test I never had. Include a request for an itemized bill, a correction, and a response within 30 days. Leave a space for my account number.
```
When to use this: A bill has a charge you don't recognize and you want it fixed in writing.

### 37
```
I want to teach my 9-year-old about money. Design a simple allowance system: how much, what it's for, how to split it between spending, saving, and giving, and 2 fun ways to show how saving grows over time.
```
When to use this: Your kid is old enough for an allowance and you want it to actually teach something.

### 38
```
I have weekends free and I'm good at [cooking / fixing things / organizing / writing — pick yours]. Suggest 5 realistic side hustles that fit those skills, what each one could pay roughly, and what I'd need to start each one this month.
```
When to use this: You want extra income and need ideas matched to what you're actually good at.

### 39
```
Help me write a for-sale listing for my used couch: 3 years old, beige fabric, no stains or damage, smoke-free pet-free home, paid $900 new. Suggest a fair asking price, a catchy title, and the details buyers want to know.
```
When to use this: You're selling furniture or stuff online and want a listing that actually gets replies.

### 40
```
Make a 7-day dinner meal plan for a family of 4 with a strict $120/week grocery budget. Every meal must be filling and kid-friendly, and end with one organized grocery list with estimated prices so I can see it fits the budget.
```
When to use this: Groceries are eating your budget and you need a week of meals that fits a number.

## Health (Prompts 41–60)

### 41
```
I have a doctor's appointment next week about my blood pressure. Help me prepare: list 10 smart questions to ask, what information to bring (medications, readings, history), and how to describe my symptoms clearly so I don't forget anything in the room.
```
When to use this: You have an appointment coming up and always think of your questions in the parking lot afterward.

### 42
```
My doctor said I have high cholesterol and mentioned LDL and HDL. Explain in plain English what those are, what the numbers mean, and what lifestyle changes actually help — no jargon, no scare tactics.
```
When to use this: You left the doctor's office with a new term you don't understand and want it explained simply.

### 43
```
Make me a beginner walking plan: I'm 55, out of shape, and currently walk almost never. Build a 4-week plan starting at 10 minutes a day, 3 days a week, gradually increasing — including what to do if my knees ache and when to take rest days.
```
When to use this: You want to start exercising but "just go for a run" isn't happening.

### 44
```
I sit at a desk all day and my lower back is stiff by 3pm. Give me 6 simple stretches I can do at my desk or in a small office, each taking under a minute, with plain-English instructions for each one.
```
When to use this: Desk work is wrecking your back and you need quick fixes, not a gym membership.

### 45
```
Suggest 10 healthy snacks I can pack in my kids' school lunches (ages 6 and 9). They must need no heating, survive in a lunchbox until noon, and be things picky eaters will actually eat. Include approximate prep time for each.
```
When to use this: Lunch-packing is a daily grind and you're out of ideas that aren't junk food.

### 46
```
I fall asleep fine but wake up at 3am every night and can't get back to sleep. Give me a practical plan: what to change in my evening routine, what to do when I wake up at 3am, and what habits make it worse.
```
When to use this: Your sleep is broken and you want real habits to try, not "just relax."

### 47
```
Explain food nutrition labels in plain English. Tell me the 4 numbers that matter most, what "daily value %" actually means, and 3 tricks food companies use to make products look healthier than they are.
```
When to use this: You read labels but the numbers mean nothing and "low fat" never seems to help.

### 48
```
Plan one week of simple healthy dinners for 1 person. I can cook basics but I hate complicated recipes and I hate wasting food. Each meal should use overlapping ingredients, take under 30 minutes, and include a grocery list.
```
When to use this: You want to eat better but cooking for one always ends in wasted groceries.

### 49
```
My knees ache when I go up and down stairs. I'm 60 and not athletic. Suggest 5 gentle, low-impact exercises to strengthen the muscles around my knees, with clear instructions and what pain means "stop and see a doctor."
```
When to use this: Your knees complain daily and you want safe, gentle movement — not a boot camp.

### 50
```
I never drink enough water. Make me a simple daily hydration plan: how much to aim for, how to spread it through the day, 5 tricks to remember (like phone alarms or a marked bottle), and which drinks actually count.
```
When to use this: You know you should drink more water but by evening you've had one glass.

### 51
```
Give me 5 different 5-minute calm-down routines for stressful days: one breathing exercise, one stretching routine, one you can do at your desk, one for right before bed, and one for when you're stuck in traffic. Simple steps for each.
```
When to use this: Stress is piling up and you need quick resets that fit into a real day.

### 52
```
Make me a healthy grocery list for one week for 2 adults on a tight budget. Focus on cheap nutritious staples, tell me which fresh produce is cheapest right now in general, and which "health foods" aren't worth the money.
```
When to use this: You want to eat healthy but assume it costs too much — it doesn't have to.

### 53
```
I want to quit smoking. Make me a 4-week quit plan: how to prepare in week 1, what to expect each week after quitting, what to do when cravings hit hard, and free resources I can call or visit for support.
```
When to use this: You're ready to quit and want a day-by-day plan plus backup for the hard moments.

### 54
```
I'm having a minor outpatient procedure next month and I'm nervous. Tell me what questions to ask my doctor beforehand, what to pack for the day, and what the recovery at home usually looks like so I can prepare my house.
```
When to use this: A procedure is scheduled and the unknown is scarier than the thing itself.

### 55
```
My pharmacist gave me a new prescription and the instruction leaflet is confusing. Explain in plain English: what this type of medicine generally does, why timing and food matter, what "take with food" really means, and what questions I should ask my pharmacist tomorrow.
```
When to use this: New medication, confusing leaflet — you want to understand it before you call back.

### 56
```
My daughter was just diagnosed with a peanut allergy. Give me 10 safe lunchbox-friendly snack swaps, explain how to read labels for hidden peanut warnings, and what to tell her school in writing.
```
When to use this: A new allergy diagnosis turned your kitchen upside down overnight.

### 57
```
I work from home at a kitchen table and my neck and shoulders hurt. Give me a checklist for setting up a comfortable workspace with what I already own (books, pillows, boxes), plus 3 posture habits to build this week.
```
When to use this: Your "home office" is a table and your body is filing complaints.

### 58
```
List exactly what should be in a home first-aid kit for a family with kids: be specific about items and quantities, what each thing is for, and what I can skip because it just takes up space.
```
When to use this: Your "first-aid kit" is a box of old bandages and you want a real one.

### 59
```
Give me 7 healthy breakfast ideas that take 5 minutes or less on a weekday morning, need no special ingredients, and will actually keep me full until lunch. No smoothies that take 20 minutes to clean up.
```
When to use this: You skip breakfast or grab junk because mornings are chaos.

### 60
```
Plan an active Saturday for my family (2 adults, kids 8 and 12) within 30 minutes of home. Suggest 3 options at different energy levels — easy, medium, adventurous — with what to bring for each and approximate costs.
```
When to use this: You want a fun active family day but "go do something" never turns into a plan.

## Work (Prompts 61–80)

### 61
```
Write a professional email asking my manager for a raise. I've been here 2 years, taken on training new hires, and my last review was excellent. Keep it confident but not demanding, and suggest a meeting to discuss it rather than naming a number in the email.
```
When to use this: You've earned a raise and need the words to ask for it without sounding awkward.

### 62
```
Write a short, professional resignation letter. I'm leaving on good terms after 3 years, my last day will be [DATE], and I want to thank my boss and offer to help with the transition. Keep it to one short paragraph plus the basics.
```
When to use this: You're leaving a job and want to exit gracefully with the right words.

### 63
```
Turn these job duties into strong resume bullet points: I answered phones, helped customers with returns, trained 3 new cashiers, and closed the store at night. Make each one start with an action verb and sound impressive but honest.
```
When to use this: Your resume reads flat and you need your real work to sound as good as it was.

### 64
```
Write a cover letter for a customer service representative job at a bank. I have 4 years of retail experience, I'm great with difficult customers, and I'm good with numbers. Keep it under 250 words and make it sound like a real person, not a template.
```
When to use this: A job posting asks for a cover letter and you're staring at a blank page.

### 65
```
I have a job interview next Tuesday for an office manager position. Give me the 8 most common interview questions for this role, a strong sample answer for each, and 3 smart questions I can ask them at the end.
```
When to use this: An interview is coming and you want to walk in prepared, not panicked.

### 66
```
Write a thank-you email to send after a job interview. I interviewed with [NAME] for [POSITION] today, we talked about [ONE THING YOU DISCUSSED], and I want to sound enthusiastic but not desperate. Keep it short.
```
When to use this: The interview went well and you want a polished follow-up in their inbox tonight.

### 67
```
I made a mistake at work — I sent a report to a client with the wrong numbers. Write a short apology email to my manager that takes responsibility, explains what happened in one sentence, says how I'll fix it, and doesn't make excuses.
```
When to use this: You messed up at work and need to own it professionally before it festers.

### 68
```
I'm running a 30-minute team meeting next Monday about our holiday schedule. Write an agenda: 3 topics max, time for each, and one clear decision we need to make before we leave the room.
```
When to use this: You're leading a meeting and want it short, focused, and actually productive.

### 69
```
Summarize this long email thread for me. Give me: what the original issue was, what each person said in one line, what was decided, and what still needs an answer. [Paste the thread.]
```
When to use this: You've been CC'd on 47 emails and have no idea what's actually going on.

### 70
```
My boss keeps piling on extra projects and I'm drowning. Write a polite email that says I can't take on the new project without dropping something else, and asks which of my current tasks is the lowest priority. Firm but professional.
```
When to use this: You're overloaded and need to push back without sounding like you're complaining.

### 71
```
Write a draft email I can send to my former boss asking for a reference letter. Remind them I worked there from [YEARS] as [ROLE], mention 2 things I did well, and make it easy for them to say yes by offering to send bullet points.
```
When to use this: You need a reference and want to make it effortless for the person writing it.

### 72
```
I took 2 years off work to care for a parent. Help me explain this gap on my resume and in interviews: one confident sentence for the resume, and a 30-second spoken answer that sounds positive, not defensive.
```
When to use this: There's a gap in your work history and you want to address it head-on with confidence.

### 73
```
Write a LinkedIn "About" section for me: I'm a [YOUR JOB TITLE] with [X] years of experience in [FIELD], known for [2 STRENGTHS]. Make it sound professional but human — no buzzwords like "synergy" or "thought leader."
```
When to use this: Your LinkedIn profile is empty or embarrassing and recruiters might actually look at it.

### 74
```
Write a professional email asking my manager if I can work from home 2 days a week. Mention my productivity, how I'd stay reachable, and propose a 1-month trial so it feels low-risk for them to say yes.
```
When to use this: You want flexible work and need a pitch that answers your boss's objections before they raise them.

### 75
```
A coworker takes credit for my work in meetings. Help me write a calm, professional message to my manager about it: describe 2 specific examples factually, say how it affects me, and ask for advice on handling it. No drama, just direct.
```
When to use this: A workplace problem needs addressing and you want to sound reasonable, not emotional.

### 76
```
I get the same 5 questions by email every week at work. Write me 5 short, professional reply templates I can copy-paste: one for "what's the status of my order," one for "can I get a refund," one for "I need this urgently," and two more common ones you suggest.
```
When to use this: You're typing the same answers over and over and want your time back.

### 77
```
Here's my to-do list for this week: [paste it]. Organize it into a day-by-day plan from Monday to Friday. Put the hardest tasks in the mornings, group similar tasks together, and tell me honestly what probably won't get done so I can move it now.
```
When to use this: Your week is a wall of tasks and you need someone to turn it into a plan.

### 78
```
Write a concise project update email to my team. The project is [NAME], we're on track / behind by one week [pick one], here's what got done this week (3 bullets), here's what's next (2 bullets), and here's the one thing I need from the team.
```
When to use this: It's update time and you want to sound organized in five minutes flat.

### 79
```
Suggest 8 team event ideas for 12 coworkers with mixed ages, a $300 total budget, and no one who wants to do trust falls. Include 2 virtual-friendly options since 3 people work remotely. Give a one-line description and rough cost for each.
```
When to use this: You're in charge of the team event and "happy hour again" isn't cutting it.

### 80
```
I need to learn basic Excel for my new job. Teach me the 10 most useful things first: simple formulas like SUM and AVERAGE, sorting, and one beginner mistake to avoid with each. Explain each one like I've never opened a spreadsheet.
```
When to use this: A new job expects spreadsheet skills and YouTube tutorials assume too much.

## Fun (Prompts 81–100)

### 81
```
Plan a 7th birthday party for my dinosaur-loving daughter: 10 kids, 2 hours, Saturday afternoon. Give me a timeline, 4 games with simple instructions, an easy cake idea, and food that kids will actually eat.
```
When to use this: A kid's birthday is coming and Pinterest is giving you anxiety instead of ideas.

### 82
```
Suggest 10 date night ideas for under $50 total. We've been together 12 years and are bored of dinner-and-a-movie. Include 3 at-home ideas, 3 outdoors, and 4 around town — and make at least 2 of them a little adventurous.
```
When to use this: Date night has gone stale and you want fresh ideas that don't cost a fortune.

### 83
```
I loved these 3 books: [list them]. Recommend 5 more books I'd probably love, and for each one tell me in one sentence why it fits my taste. No classics I've already heard of a hundred times.
```
When to use this: You finished a great book and need your next one to be just as good.

### 84
```
It's Friday family movie night. We have kids ages 6 and 10, and the adults are tired. Suggest 5 movies everyone will enjoy, tell me where each one is streaming, and flag any scary or sad parts so there are no surprises.
```
When to use this: It's 7pm, everyone's hungry, and nobody can agree on a movie.

### 85
```
Plan a 3-day weekend road trip within 4 hours of [YOUR CITY] for 2 adults who like small towns, good food, and scenic drives. Give me a route, 2 overnight stop options at different price points, and one "don't miss" thing per day.
```
When to use this: You want to get out of town this weekend but planning feels like work.

### 86
```
I want to start a small vegetable garden but I've never grown anything. I have a 4x8 foot sunny spot in my yard. Tell me the 5 easiest vegetables for a beginner, when to plant each one, and the basic care each needs per week.
```
When to use this: You want to grow your own food and need someone to tell you exactly where to start.

### 87

### 89

### 90
